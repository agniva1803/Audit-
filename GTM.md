# Go-to-Market Strategy

## Exact target user

**Engineering Manager or CTO at a 5–25 person Series A startup, 6–18 months post-funding.**

More specifically: someone who signed off on "just use whatever AI tools you need" during the hiring push, now looking at a $2k–$8k/month AI line item in their P&L and having trouble justifying it to their CFO. They didn't buy these tools through a procurement process — they accreted organically as engineers added subscriptions. They have no dashboard, no policy, and no benchmark.

Job title on LinkedIn: "CTO," "VP Engineering," "Head of Engineering." Company stage: Series A, 10–50 employees. Industries: B2B SaaS, developer tools, fintech.

## What they Google or scroll through before wanting this

- "cursor vs copilot which is better 2026"
- "how much does github copilot cost per developer"
- "ai tools for engineering team budget"
- "claude api vs chatgpt api cost comparison"
- Scrolling the Hacker News front page on a Wednesday morning
- Reading the "Stack" section of a founder newsletter (Lenny's, The Pragmatic Engineer)

## Where they hang out online

- **r/ExperiencedDevs** and **r/cscareerquestions** — budget and tooling questions surface here
- **Hacker News** — "Ask HN: How much does your team spend on AI tools?" posts get hundreds of comments
- **Indie Hackers Slack** — #tools channel; founders openly share what they pay
- **The Pragmatic Engineer Discord** — engineering leadership subscribers
- **X/Twitter** — following @amasad, @swyx, @levelsio, @karpathy — the AI builder crowd
- **Ship It! / Software Engineering Daily Slack** — tech leads

## First 100 users in 30 days, $0 budget

**Week 1 — Seed with HN:**
Write an "Ask HN: We built a free AI spend auditor for engineering teams" post. The hook is the honesty: "We built it for our own lead-gen but it's a genuinely useful free tool — here's the methodology." HN respects transparency. Realistic outcome: 200–500 visitors, 40–80 audits, 10–20 emails captured.

**Week 1 — Post the results publicly:**
Screenshot the most interesting audit (e.g., "team spending $800/mo on Cursor Business for 2 engineers — here's what we found") as a Twitter/X thread. No product mention in the first 3 tweets — lead with the insight. Credex as a footnote.

**Week 2 — Direct outreach:**
Search LinkedIn for "VP Engineering" + "Series A" + location. DM 20/day with: "Hey [name] — we built a free 2-min audit for AI tool spend. Found $1,200/mo in savings for a team similar in size to yours. Worth 2 minutes?" Expect 3–5% response rate → 2–3 audits/day from this channel.

**Week 2 — Indie Hackers post:**
Post in #products with the methodology explained. IH readers are the target user and love tools built with transparency about business model.

**Week 3 — Newsletter drops:**
Cold email 10 founder-facing newsletters (The Pragmatic Engineer, TLDR, Software Lead Weekly). Pitch: "Free tool for your audience, here's the data story." Even 1 pickup = 500–1000 visitors.

**Week 4 — Reddit:**
Answer 5 existing threads on r/ExperiencedDevs and r/SaaS about AI tool costs — don't plug the tool, just give useful advice. Add the tool link to profile. Karma builds, tool gets surfaced organically.

## Unfair distribution channel

Credex has access to companies that bought AI credits and may have overpurchased. Reaching out to those companies with "here's a free audit of your current retail spend — here's what we found" is an extremely warm intro. No competitor has this channel because no competitor has the credit inventory list.

## Week-1 traction if this works

- 300+ unique visitors from HN post
- 80+ audits completed
- 25+ emails captured
- 3–5 consultation booking requests from high-savings cases (>$500/mo)
- 1 piece of press/newsletter coverage initiated
